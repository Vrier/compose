# Chapter 7 · Adjective type-shifting (MOD)

The chapter 7 adjective denotations by a second route. §7.2 gives it in a
footnote: an equivalent result is achievable without a new composition rule,
via a type-shifting rule. Type-shifting is taken up in earnest at §7.4.1.

## 7.2 The MOD type-shift

**MOD** (§7.2): maps an adjective `α′` of type `⟨e,t⟩` to
`λP . λx . [α′(x) ∧ P(x)]`, type `⟨⟨e,t⟩,⟨e,t⟩⟩`, so that it combines with the
noun by FA. The truth conditions match the PM route, and the book states that
nothing in what follows depends on choosing one over the other.

Engine note: the book writes ⇑NAME on a node where a type-shift has applied;
here MOD applies at the AP node and the bracketing does not change.

Group A: apply MOD to the adjective, then FA with the noun.

Group B: each adjective undergoes MOD in turn and every step is FA. The
innermost shifted adjective takes the bare noun, the outer one takes the
result. The third item adds negation.

## 7.1 Non-intersective adjectives

*alleged* and *former* are neither intersective nor subsective, and the book
gives them type `⟨⟨e,t⟩,⟨e,t⟩⟩` composing by FA. In this set they are lexically
that type, so they never start at `⟨e,t⟩` and MOD has nothing to apply to.

MOD's inputs are the adjectives that also work predicatively, as in *Pippin is
mischievous*. The non-subsective ones resist that frame: *a former millionaire*
entails neither *a millionaire* nor *\*Frida is former* (§7.1, Exercise 1).

Group C: take the nominal predicate directly by FA, with no shift.
