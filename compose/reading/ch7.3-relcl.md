# Chapter 7 · Relative Clauses

A relative clause denotes an `⟨e,t⟩` predicate and joins its head noun by PM
(§7.2).

## 7.3 Variable binding at LF

Three assumptions (§7.3): traces and pronouns are variables, interpreted
relative to an assignment; syntax supplies the co-indexation; a binder node
triggers abstraction. Traces: `tᵢ` denotes `xᵢ` (§7.3.1). **PA**: a node
`[i β]` denotes the function taking `x` to `⟦β⟧` with `xᵢ` set to `x`.

Engine note: the LP node in the trees is the book's λᵢ binder; *who/which* are
vacuous (`λX . X`) and *that* is `λp . p`, so the abstract passes up unchanged.

## 7.3.1 Relative clauses

Subject gaps (Group A): the trace sits in subject position, so the movement is
string-vacuous, and PA turns the clause into the predicate the noun needs.

Object gaps (Group B): the subject composes with the verb first and the trace
fills the object slot; PA over the index yields the predicate, and PM conjoins
it with the head noun.

Full sentences (Group C): with *the*, the description presupposes a unique
satisfier of noun-plus-clause; compose clause → PM → article → sentence.
