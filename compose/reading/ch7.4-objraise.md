# Chapter 7 · Quantifiers in object position

A quantifier phrase is `⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩` and a transitive verb wants type `e`. In
object position neither can take the other as an argument. §7.3.2 repairs this
by movement; the in-situ type-shifting repair is the RaiseO/RaiseS set.

## 7.3.2 Quantifier raising

**QR** (§7.3.2): a syntactic transformation moving a `⟨⟨e,t⟩,t⟩` expression to a
position where it can be interpreted, leaving a DP trace in its previous
position. It maps Surface Structure to Logical Form, the level at which
each constituent sits in the position where the semantics interprets it.
Pronunciation is fixed at Surface Structure, so the movement is covert.

At the landing site the quantifier's sister is a binder node, and it triggers
Predicate Abstraction, playing the role a relative pronoun plays in a relative
clause.

Engine note: the book's λS node with its λ1 binder appears here as the LP node
with a numeral leaf, and PA resolves it as in the relative-clause set.

Group A: raise the object DP, leaving a type-`e` trace. PA at the LP node gives
an `⟨e,t⟩` predicate, which the quantifier then takes.

Group B: with quantifiers in both positions the order of raising fixes the
scope, and each QR step adds its own LP node. Raising the object alone, subject
in situ, puts the object on top and yields inverse scope; raising the subject
above the landed object yields surface scope. The book records a preference for
surface scope when both arguments are quantificational, while QR generates both
orders (§7.3.2, Exercise 8).
