# Chapter 4 · Nonverbal predicates, modifiers, and the definite article

*Is* and *a* are vacuous, `λX . X` (§4.1). *Is a cat* does the work of *cat*.

## 4.3 Predicate Modification

**PM** (§4.3.1): if both daughters are type `⟨e,t⟩`, the node denotes the
predicate true of `x` iff both daughters are, their intersection. FA handles
everything else.

Group A: *gray cat* and *cat in Texas* compose by PM; `⟦in⟧` saturates its
object first, then the PP joins the noun by PM. Nonintersective adjectives
(*former*, *alleged*) fall outside PM (§4.3.3).

## 4.4 The definite article

**the** (§4.4.1): partial, type `⟨⟨e,t⟩,e⟩`, defined only if the predicate has
exactly one satisfier, and returning that individual. In
`λf : ∃!x[f(x)=1] . ιx[f(x)=1]`, the condition after the colon is presupposed
and the value after the dot asserted (§4.4.2). Failure leaves the sentence
neither true nor false, whereas a type mismatch is visible from the types alone
(§4.4.4).

Group B: the presupposition rides up through the vacuous copula to the whole
sentence.

## 4.5 Modifiers in definite descriptions

Compose the modifier first (PM), then the article, so *the gray cat*
presupposes uniqueness among the gray cats.

Group C: the same pipeline with *fond of Joe* and a relative-clause restrictor;
how the clause gets its `⟨e,t⟩` meaning is ch. 5 (PA).
